Buying at auction: a timetable for finance

Auction purchases run to a fixed clock. A practical sequence for arranging finance before and after the hammer falls.

22 September 20263 minute readPhoenix Capital

At a traditional property auction, the fall of the hammer creates a binding contract. Completion usually follows within a fixed period, commonly 28 days. Finance needs to be ready within that window.

Before the auction

  • Speak to your lender once you have shortlisted a lot.
  • Share the legal pack so that title and special conditions can be reviewed.
  • Agree indicative terms and, where possible, instruct a valuation.
  • Have your solicitor review the pack and be ready to act.

After the auction

Confirm the purchase with your lender straight away. With the groundwork done, the remaining steps are largely legal, and the time to completion can be used productively rather than anxiously.

Modern method auctions

Some auctions operate on a reservation basis with a longer period to exchange and complete. The same principle applies: arrange finance early, and read the terms of sale carefully.

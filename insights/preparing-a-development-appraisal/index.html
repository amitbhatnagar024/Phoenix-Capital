Funding a development: preparing a credible appraisal

A considered appraisal is the foundation of any development facility. What we expect to see, and the assumptions we test.

8 October 20265 minute readPhoenix Capital

A development appraisal is the single most important document in a funding request. It sets out what the scheme will cost, what it will be worth and how much margin there is between the two.

The core elements

  • Gross development value. The expected value of the completed scheme, supported by comparable sales or a valuer's opinion.
  • Land and acquisition costs. Including stamp duty, legal and agency fees.
  • Build costs. Ideally from a quantity surveyor or a priced tender, with a contingency.
  • Professional fees and finance costs. Often underestimated in early drafts.
  • Programme. Construction time plus a realistic sales or refinance period.

The assumptions we test

We look carefully at values, costs and time. Small changes in any of the three can have a significant effect on profit. A scheme that remains viable under modest stress is one we can support with confidence.

Presenting the team

Alongside the numbers, we want to understand who is delivering the project: the developer's track record, the contractor and the professional team. Experience is valuable, but so is a well-chosen team around a less experienced developer.

We welcome early conversations on schemes at the appraisal stage.
